Fire Detector Guide

Selection, spacing, and placement regulations for fire detectors per NFPA 72, BS 5839-1, and SNI.

📡 Smoke Detector

  • Coverage: 81 m² per unit (9m × 9m grid) for ceiling height ≤ 6 m.
  • Spacing: Maximum 9.1 m between detectors; within 4.55 m of any wall.
  • Best suited: Offices, hotels, residential, hospitals, electronic rooms.
  • Avoid: Kitchens, bathrooms, dusty/humid areas, near HVAC vents (min 1m away).
  • Temperature range: 0°C to 38°C operating.
  • Types: Ionization (fast flaming), Photoelectric (smoldering), Aspirating (VESDA - high sensitivity).

🌡️ Heat Detector

  • Coverage: 50 m² per unit (7m × 7m grid) for ceiling height ≤ 6 m.
  • Spacing: Maximum 6.7 m between detectors.
  • Types: Fixed temperature (57°C/79°C/93°C) or Rate-of-Rise (ROR).
  • Best suited: Kitchens, boiler rooms, garages, dusty industrial areas where smoke detectors false-trigger.
  • Max ceiling height: 7.5 m for point type; use linear/beam type for higher.

🔥 Flame Detector (UV/IR)

  • Use: Flammable liquid/gas storage, aircraft hangars, fuel loading bays.
  • Coverage: Cone of vision up to 30-60 m depending on fuel.
  • Response: Sub-second detection of open flames.

🧲 Manual Call Point (MCP / Break Glass)

  • Location: At all final exits, stairwell exits on each floor, escape routes.
  • Spacing: Max 45 m travel distance between MCPs (BS 5839), or 30 m (NFPA where audibility limited).
  • Mounting height: 1.4 m (±200 mm) from floor to operating element.
  • Color: Red (Europe/Asia); White with red lettering (USA).
  • Must be: Visible, unobstructed, weatherproof if external.

🔊 Sounders & Visual Indicators

  • Min SPL: 65 dB(A) throughout, or 5 dB above ambient (max 120 dB).
  • Sleeping areas: 75 dB(A) minimum at bedhead.
  • Visual strobes: Required in noisy areas (>105 dB ambient), hearing-impaired areas, and per ADA.
  • Sounder spacing: Typical 1 per 60-100 m² depending on construction.
  • Tone: Distinct from other building signals; AS/NZS requires alternating 440Hz/525Hz in residential.
Special Spacing Adjustments:
  • Ceiling height > 6 m → reduce coverage by 20% per additional 1 m.
  • Beamed ceilings (beam depth > 10% of ceiling height) → detector every beam pocket if beams < 1.2 m apart.
  • Corridors → spacing may be increased along corridor axis, max 9.1 m between detectors.
  • High airflow rooms → additional detectors, mount ≥ 3× air change distance from supply diffuser.

APAR (Alat Pemadam Api Ringan) Guide

Panduan seleksi, penempatan, dan pemeliharaan APAR berdasarkan SNI 03-3989-2000 dan Peraturan Dinas Pemadam Kebakaran Indonesia.

🧯 Dry Chemical Powder (DCP) - ABC Type

  • Gunanya untuk: Class A (Padat), B (Cair), C (Listrik) - Sangat serbaguna.
  • Ukuran standar SNI: 3 kg, 6 kg, 9 kg (Portable); 12 kg, 25 kg, 50 kg (Trolley/Roda).
  • Isi: Monoammonium phosphate (bubuk kuning).
  • Jangkauan semprot: 3-5 meter untuk unit portable.
  • Waktu semprot: 8-25 detik (tergantung ukuran).
  • Tekanan: Bertekanan dalam (Stored pressure) dengan gas Nitrogen (N2).

📏 Persyaratan Penempatan (SNI 03-3989-2000)

  • Jarak Tempuh (Travel Distance): Maksimal 20 m (Ringan), 15 m (Sedang), 10 m (Tinggi).
  • Ketinggian Pemasangan: Handle pegangan antara 1.0 m s/d 1.5 m dari lantai.
  • Jarak dari lantai: Bagian bawah APAR minimal 100 mm dari lantai.
  • Visibilitas: Harus terlihat jelas; wajib ada rambu (tanda) "APAR" jika tertutup oleh furnitur/pilar.
  • Aksesibilitas: Tidak boleh di belakang pintu terkunci; harus di jalur normal orang berjalan.
  • Lokasi Strategis: Dekat pintu keluar, tangga darurat, dan di pintu masuk area bahaya.

    🔧 Inspeksi & Pemeliharaan (Maintenance)

    • Bulanan: Pengecekan visual oleh petugas K3/Gedung - posisi, akses, tekanan manometer (harus di zona hijau), segel tidak rusak.
    • Tahunan: Inspeksi & perawatan oleh Vendor/Kontraktor Pemadam yang bersertifikat. Wajib diberi kartu/label perawatan.

    • Uji Tekan (Hydrostatic Test): Setiap 5 tahun untuk tabung DCP.
    • Penggantian: Segera ganti jika tabung berkarat parah, manometer rusak, atau sudah 10 tahun sejak produksi.
    🚫 JANGAN gunakan DCP pada:
    • Kebakaran Logam (Class D - butuh agent khusus Class D)
    • Deep Fryer Minyak Goreng (Class K / F - gunakan Wet Chemical / Foam)
    • Elektronik presisi (Server/Data Center - gunakan Gas/Clean Agent, karena sisa bubuk DCP korosif dan merusak sirkuit)

    Hydrant System Guide

    Design and installation requirements per NFPA 13/14, SNI 03-1745-2000, and Permen PU No.26/2008.

    💧 System Components

    • Water supply: City main, dedicated tank (min. 2-hour reserve), or natural source.
    • Fire pump: Electric/diesel driven; sized for max demand + 10% margin.
    • Jockey pump: Maintains system pressure to prevent fire pump cycling.
    • Underground/overhead piping: Loop or grid for redundancy.
    • Hydrant pillars: Wet or dry barrel; double-outlet type standard.
    • Hose reels/cabinets: 19mm-25mm rubber hose, 30m length, 1.5 bar nozzle.
    • Siamese connection (FDC): For fire department pumper to supplement system.

    📍 Hydrant Spacing & Coverage

    • Indoor hydrant spacing: Max 38 m between pillars (NFPA 14).
    • Hose coverage: Every point reachable by 30 m hose + 6 m spray.
    • Outdoor hydrants (yard): Spaced max 90 m apart (Indonesian SNI).
    • Distance to building: Max 60 m from any building entry point.
    • Distance from roads: Max 7.5 m for fire department access.

    ⚡ Pressure & Flow Requirements

    Hose Reel (25mm)1.5 bar30 LPM
    Hydrant Pillar (2 outlets)2.5 bar500 LPM per outlet
    Monitor / Water Cannon5.0 bar1200 LPM

    Standpipe (Class II)4.5 bar at top outlet100 GPM (378 LPM)

    🔧 Pump Selection Criteria

    • Rated flow: Equal to system demand (sum of simultaneous outlets + hose streams).
    • Rated head: Overcome static lift + friction loss + residual pressure at hydraulically remote outlet.
    • No overload: At 150% of rated flow, pump head ≥ 65% of rated head.
    • Shutoff: Head at zero flow ≤ 140% rated.
    • Power supply: Diesel driver preferred for reliability; electric pump requires backup generator.
    • Controller: Automatic start on pressure drop; manual reset required.

    🧪 Testing & Maintenance

    • Weekly: Jockey pump run, fire pump churn test (no-flow) for 10 min.
    • Monthly: Pump house inspection, control valve position checks.
    • Quarterly: Main drain test, alarm valve trip test.
    • Annual: Full flow test, hydrostatic test of new piping, fire pump performance curve verification.
    • 5-year: Internal inspection of valves, sprinkler sample testing.
    ⚠️ Common Compliance Issues:
    • Insufficient water tank reserve (must sustain 2 hr for Ordinary Hazard, 3-4 hr for High Hazard).
    • Under-sized diesel fuel day tank (min. 8 hours of operation required).
    • Lack of jockey pump causing fire pump short cycling.
    • Pipework not looped - single line failures take out entire system.
    • Missing or inaccessible FDC (Siamese) connections.

    Evacuation & Emergency Systems Guide

    Comprehensive guidance on evacuation routes, emergency doors, lighting, and exit signage per NFPA 101, BS 5266, ISO 16069.

    🚪 Emergency Exit Doors

    • Minimum width: 0.9 m clear opening (1.2 m for high occupancy).
    • Minimum height: 2.0 m.
    • Door swing: Open outward in direction of escape travel.
    • Panic hardware: Required for Assembly (A) / Educational (E) / Hazardous (H) occupancies - panic bar/latch operable with single motion.
    • Self-closing & latching: Required for fire-rated doors (minimum FD30 / 30 min fire rating).
    • Number of exits: Min 2 from every floor/room; more based on occupant load.
    • Travel distance to exit: 30 m unsprinklered / 45 m sprinklered (ordinary hazard).
    • Common path of travel: Max 12-22 m depending on occupancy (single direction before reaching two exits).
    • Locks: Never lock from inside; electromagnetic locks must release on fire alarm.

    🏃 Evacuation Routes

    • Min corridor width: 1.1 m; 1.5 m for high occupancy.
    • Stairway width: Min 1.1 m between handrails.
    • Stair riser/tread: 180 mm max riser, 280 mm min tread.
    • Max travel distance:
      • Office/Retail (sprinklered): 45 m
      • Office/Retail (unsprinklered): 30 m
      • Industrial (sprinklered): 90 m
      • Assembly (sprinklered): 60 m
    • Discharge: Exit must lead directly to exterior or protected stairway.
    • Smoke-free escape: Protected shafts require pressurization or natural smoke ventilation.
    • Aisles: Min 1.0 m clear; locked seating rows not permitted.

    💡 Emergency Lighting

    • Required locations: All escape routes, stairways, changes of direction, fire-fighting equipment, exit doors, control rooms.
    • Illuminance level:
      • Escape routes ≤ 2 m wide: min 1.0 lux at floor level.
      • High-risk areas: min 10 lux (or 10% of normal, whichever greater).
      • Open anti-panic areas: min 0.5 lux at floor.
    • Duration: Min 1 hour for small buildings; 3 hours for large/high-rise/sleeping risk.
    • Response time: Must reach 50% illuminance within 5 sec, 100% within 60 sec.
    • Mounting height: Min 2.0 m above floor for self-contained luminaires.
    • Power source: Central battery system or individual battery packs; charged from mains and tested monthly.

    🪧 Exit Signs & Lamps

    • Visibility: From any point in escape route, a sign must be visible and unambiguous.
    • Color: Green background with white symbol/lettering (ISO 7010 / international standard). US uses red.
    • Symbol: Running man (ISO 7010 E001/E002) preferred over text-only signs.
    • Mounting height: Top of sign ≥ 2.0 m above finished floor level.
    • Illumination: Internally illuminated or externally lit to min 50 lux on sign face.
    • Max viewing distance: 200 × panel height (m) for internally illuminated signs.
    • Battery backup: Same duration as emergency lighting (1-3 hr).
    • Directional arrows: Indicate direction to nearest exit; chevrons point toward exit at final exit.
    • Photoluminescent: Permitted only with continuous charging light source ≥ 50 lux.

    ✅ Evacuation Best Practices:
    • Conduct evacuation drills at least every 6 months (annually for low-risk buildings).
    • Maintain assembly points at safe distance (≥ 18 m from building).
    • Post evacuation floor plans at every floor entrance and elevator lobby.
    • Designate and train floor wardens (1 per 20 occupants).
    • Account for Persons with Reduced Mobility (PRM) via refuge areas with 2-way communication.
    • Test emergency lighting and exit signs monthly; record results in logbook.
